# Interview · sources

The method is cited; no source material is reproduced.

## What it takes from each field

| Field | What it brings to the method |
|---|---|
| **Business process management** (process discovery) | interview-based discovery with the people who run the process; knowledge is fragmented across roles, so each answer records who gave it; validate before modelling; iterate instead of asking everything at once |
| **Six Sigma** (SIPOC) | the high-level view of a process before its steps: suppliers, inputs, process, outputs, customers. Round 2 asks for each column, then for the steps |
| **Requirements engineering** (elicitation) | closed questions with options for facts, open questions for steps and paths; stakeholders cannot always say what they know; record the source of every requirement so it can be traced and confirmed |
| **Survey methodology** (questionnaire design) | short, concrete questions; exhaustive and mutually clear options; an explicit «don't know» option so an unknown is not forced into a wrong answer; the same wording every time so answers stay comparable |

## Design choices that are not in the books

- **Batches of 3 to 4.** The upper limit is the AskUserQuestion tool (4 questions per call, 4
  options per question). The lower limit keeps the number of turns small; the last batch of a round
  may be shorter.
- **A fixed bank.** The questions live in a file and are never regenerated by the model: the same
  wording every session (comparable answers, a record that matches back to its question) and fewer
  tokens in the conversation.
- **The gap with its date.** «No sé» is recorded and dated so the next session asks the right person
  again, instead of the manual filling the hole with a plausible guess.
